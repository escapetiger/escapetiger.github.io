Poisson Manifolds

Abstract. A smooth manifold tells us what it means to move and differentiate, but it does not determine a dynamics. A Poisson structure adds an antisymmetric bracket on smooth functions that turns each function into a vector field and each Hamiltonian into an evolution law. This post introduces that idea from canonical Hamiltonian mechanics, passes from the algebraic bracket to the intrinsic Poisson bivector, explains the Jacobi identity and its coordinate form, and shows how a possibly degenerate Poisson manifold decomposes into symplectic leaves. The main examples are canonical phase space, two-dimensional Poisson structures, and the Lie–Poisson structure on $\mathbb{R}^3$.

Smooth Manifolds

Abstract. A smooth manifold is a space that may be globally curved or topologically complicated while remaining locally indistinguishable from Euclidean space. This post introduces the subject from scratch, with a notation guide and a sequence of geometric illustrations. We begin with topology, charts, transition maps, and atlases; then define smooth functions and smooth maps without assuming that the manifold sits inside a larger Euclidean space. From there, we construct tangent and cotangent spaces, differentials, vector fields, integral curves, flows, and Lie brackets. The sphere serves as a recurring example, allowing each abstract definition to be compared with familiar geometry. The central theme is that coordinates make calculations possible, while compatibility under coordinate changes makes the resulting mathematics intrinsic.

Korn's Inequality

Abstract. In elasticity and viscous-fluid mechanics, energy controls the symmetric part of a vector-field gradient, whereas compactness and well-posedness usually require control of the full gradient. Korn’s inequality bridges this gap after the invisible rigid motions have been removed. This post explains the geometric obstruction, states the principal bounded-domain estimates, and gives a detailed proof of the quotient form using the Poincaré–Lions inequality in $H^{-1}$.

The Gelfand Triple and Weak Laplacian

Abstract. The classical Laplacian asks for two derivatives, but weak solutions of elliptic and parabolic equations often possess only one square-integrable derivative. This post introduces the canonical Gelfand triple $H_0^1(\Omega) \hookrightarrow L^2(\Omega) \hookrightarrow H^{-1}(\Omega)$ from scratch. We explain what each embedding means, construct the weak Dirichlet Laplacian as a bounded map from $H_0^1(\Omega)$ to $H^{-1}(\Omega)$, distinguish it from the unbounded Laplacian on $L^2(\Omega)$, and show why this framework is natural for the Poisson and heat equations.
